# r2 — Selection reliability: why the D≤30 cells are untrustworthy

Study cover (question, verdict, provenance): [../../README.md](../../README.md).

## Question

Every held-out number in this study comes from the checkpoint with the best
*within-subject* validation likelihood (`best_eval`). At small training cohorts
the D≤30 cells behave erratically — the single-block arms even fall below the
no-added-inputs baseline. Is that a real effect, or a measurement artifact of the
checkpoint-selection rule? This report is the diagnostic that settles it, and the
reason the D≤30 cells are excluded from every estimate in r1.

*Held-out scoring uses the best-validation checkpoint (`best_eval` = argmax within-subject `checkpoint/eval_likelihood`). This panel shows why that selection is unreliable below D≈100.*

- **The selection signal is broken at small cohorts.** corr(within-subject, held-out) across seeds rises from **-0.00** at D=10 to **1.00** at D=614. Below D≈100 the within-subject metric `best_eval` selects on is essentially uncorrelated with what we report, so it cannot pick a good checkpoint.
- **Seed noise dwarfs the effect at small D.** At D=10 the held-out seed sd is **0.0282** and the within-subject sd **0.0877** — both far above the ~0.0075 effect; by D≥100 they fall below it.
- **Consequence.** D≤30 cells are excluded from every effect estimate (drawn as open markers). A future fix is to select checkpoints on held-out LL (`best_heldout`) rather than within-subject.

## Discussion

The mechanism is not "small cohorts overtrain and get scored past their peak" —
`best_eval` selects the best-validation checkpoint, not the final one, so that
cannot happen. The real problem is upstream of scoring: at small cohorts the
*within-subject validation signal that `best_eval` selects on* has itself
collapsed, with seed-to-seed noise larger than the effect. Selection then lands
near-arbitrarily among a set of checkpoints whose validation LL no longer tracks
held-out LL, so the reported number is dominated by which checkpoint happened to
be picked rather than by the inputs under test.

This is why the study reports everything at D≥100, where within-subject LL is
informative again and selection works (at D=614 the within-subject and held-out
rankings coincide exactly). The clean fix for a future variant is to select on
held-out LL directly (`best_heldout`), which also ties into the early-stopping
`min_delta` change already flagged as a follow-up.
