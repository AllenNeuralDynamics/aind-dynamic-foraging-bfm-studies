# r4 — Are side and engagement carried in separable subspaces?

**Question.** C3's side and engage readout directions are nearly orthogonal (cosine 0.10).
Does the network also *use* them separately — can one be removed without the other?
Mean-replacement ablations at every step, on 1,500 random eval sessions, with random
directions as controls.

## Summary — what the third action adds, and how the two are related

1. **Removing engagement leaves side prediction intact.** Clamping the engage direction to
   its mean costs L/R likelihood 0.010, within the range of random 1-d directions
   (0.001–0.019), while engagement drops by 0.139 (to 0.709, below a constant base rate of
   0.741) — 0.100 when only the carried state is clamped, so the engage direction carries
   engagement forward in time, not just to the readout.
2. **The side direction carries side memory, as in C.** Clamping it in the carried state
   only costs L/R 0.118 (full clamp: 0.239, to chance by construction).
3. **But removing side costs engagement too — the dependence is one-way.** Clamping the
   side direction costs engagement 0.049 (0.042 in the carried state), ~8× the largest
   random direction. *Inference*: the mean state is side-neutral, and a neutral side value is
   where post-ignore (disengaged) states sit (r6, I1), so the clamp partly reads as
   disengagement; not tested.

## What is by construction vs what is a finding

- **By construction.** The full clamp of a readout direction removes that readout's
  variation (side → chance L/R). Random directions set the scale of an off-manifold push.
- **Findings.** The memory-only costs (what each direction carries forward), and the
  asymmetry: engagement is removable without touching side; side is not removable without
  touching engagement.

## Caveats

- One mean for all states (the all-trials mean); Study 50's E2b conditional-mean ablations
  (mean per last input) are not repeated here.
- 1,500 sessions, one seed (C3-s0).
