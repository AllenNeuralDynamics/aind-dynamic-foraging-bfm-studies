# r5 — Engagement style in the subject embedding

**Question.** Mice differ widely in how much they ignore (per-mouse ignore rate 0.5–44%).
Does C3's subject embedding carry that engagement style, unlike C's, and which part of C3's
ignore dynamics does it set — the attractor, or how fast a mouse gets there and back?

## Summary — how C3 differs from C, and what the ignore input does

1. **C3's embedding carries engagement; C's carries none.** From the 4-d embedding, a linear
   decoder recovers each mouse's ignore rate with cross-validated R² 0.68 (0.47–0.68 across
   C3 seeds; corrected 2026-10-24, r7) (C: 0.01) and the
   rise in ignoring over a session with 0.45 (C: −0.01); bout length and the probability of
   staying disengaged are weaker (0.13, 0.15; C: ≤ 0). So engagement style is not visible
   in a mouse's L/R behaviour (C would have picked it up), and C3 has learned it as a
   separate embedding dimension.
2. **Mice differ in how quickly an ignore disengages them, not in where they end up.** At
   each mouse's own context, C3's ignore attractor is at P(ignore) 0.97–0.98 for every mouse
   (uncorrelated with behaviour). What varies is the first step: P(ignore) after one ignore
   ranges 0.03–0.58 across mice and correlates r = 0.90 with the mouse's observed ignore rate
   (0.54 with its within-session rise). After three ignores the spread narrows (0.40–0.66;
   r = 0.39–0.47 with the observed features).

## What is by construction vs what is a finding

- **By construction.** C3 is trained to fit each mouse's ignores, so *some* engagement
  information in its embedding is expected; C is not, so some absence is too. The
  per-mouse dynamics are read from the same model whose embedding is decoded.
- **Findings.** That C's embedding carries *no* engagement information at all (engagement is
  not predictable from side behaviour); that in C3 it is carried mainly by the entry speed
  into disengagement, with a common attractor across mice.

## Caveats

- Recovery after the attractor (three unrewarded choices) splits the mice into two groups
  (r = 0.35 with ignore rate); the recovery input uses the side favoured at a near-neutral
  attractor, which may decide the group. Not interpreted further.
- 300 ignore iterations leave a largest per-step change of 3e-3 (slowest τ ≈ 500 steps), so
  the attractor P(ignore) is approximate to ~0.01.
- The engagement half of phase F only; how the context moves the side attractors (Study 50
  F1–F4 for C) is not repeated for C3.
