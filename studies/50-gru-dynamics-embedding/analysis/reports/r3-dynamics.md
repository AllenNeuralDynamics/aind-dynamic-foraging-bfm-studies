# Result 3 — dynamics of model C: fixed points, timescales, flow (PLAN.md phase D)

**Scope.** Model C seed 0, five contexts (the mean context over all eval sessions, and the
mean contexts of F1's low/high-gain and low/high-bias mice), each of the four inputs
(choice × reward of the trial just completed) held constant. The step is the trained
network's own update with the context as a free input (wrapper `gru_step`), parity-checked
against the stored forward pass. Run on CPU (no GPU in the container).

## Summary

1. **One trial moves the state far; the dynamics are input-driven jumps, not slow
   accumulation.** A single step carries most states across the PC1–PC2 plane into the
   region belonging to the input just received — the four previous-choice × outcome regions
   of r1 B3c (D1).
2. **Each input has (mostly) one stable fixed point, inside its own region, on the chosen
   side.** Repeating "right, rewarded" ends at logit(right) ≈ +3.5 to +4.9 depending on
   context; repeating "right, unrewarded" also ends on the right (≈ +2.8 to +3.2): with the
   input held fixed, repeating the same choice dominates even without reward. "Left,
   rewarded" is bistable in 3 of 5 contexts — two nearby stable points with a saddle between
   them, partly off the visited manifold (PC2 ≈ −4).
3. **The slowest modes at these attractors are very slow (τ ≈ 120–650 trials; up to ≈ 1900
   at the second, bistable "left rewarded" point) but barely touch the choice readout** (overlap 0.0–0.31); they point partly along
   PC5 (time-in-session, up to 0.65) and PC2. So the constant-input linearisation does **not**
   explain the behavioural timescale (the counterfactual reward effect decays over 2–3
   trials, r2): that timescale comes from the transient jumps between regions as inputs
   alternate, not from relaxation near an attractor.
4. **Context changes attractor depth, not attractor position.** The fixed points of all five
   contexts sit at nearly the same PC1–PC2 locations (D2), but their depth along the readout
   differs in the way F3 predicts: **gain deepens both sides** (high vs low gain:
   right +4.6 vs +3.8, left −5.0 vs −4.0 for rewarded inputs) while **bias deepens one side
   and shallows the other** (high vs low bias: right +4.9 vs +3.5, left −3.9 vs −5.5).
   This is the fixed-point half of F3 for group-mean contexts. **Revised by F2 (r5 item 8),
   which covers every mouse:** across mice, attractor depth tracks persistence (ρ +0.50),
   not reward gain (ρ +0.28). Gain shows up as how fast v relaxes. D2's "gain" contexts lay
   along F3's combined gain + persistence direction, so their deeper attractors go with the
   persistence part.

## Caveats and method notes

- **First search failed silently, fixed.** A first run (Adam, fixed learning rate, float32)
  stalled at q ≈ 1e-5 for every start and reported no fixed points; the current search adds a
  decaying rate, forward iteration and float64 Newton refinement. 58–84% of candidates now
  converge; the rest end in slow regions, so additional slow points / fixed points in
  rarely-visited regions cannot be excluded.
- **Constant inputs are an unnatural regime** (e.g. choosing right forever without reward);
  the attractors describe where each input pushes the state, not states the animals occupy
  for long. Several sit at the edge of, or just beyond, the visited state cloud.
- **Near-1 eigenvalues may reflect saturated units** (update gates near closed) rather than
  functional memory; their low readout overlap is consistent with that.
- Context groups are means over sessions of 61 mice each (F1), so the depth differences are
  descriptive.

## What this suggests next

- **Trajectory linearisation (D, extension):** products of Jacobians along real trajectories
  with the actual inputs give the finite-time decay of readout-relevant perturbations — the
  quantity that should match the counterfactual kernel, and where gain should act per mouse.
- E2 / E2b (r4) and F2 (r5) have since been done.
