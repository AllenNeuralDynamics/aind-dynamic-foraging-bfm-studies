# Result 1 — first look at model C's hidden state (PLAN.md phase B)

**Scope.** One model (C seed 0: GRU H128, 4-d subject embedding, trained on 614 mice), the
training mice's eval sessions, all tasks pooled unless split by the session table's `task`.
Every number below comes from one forward pass; nothing is counterfactual yet.

## Summary

1. **The model matches the animals on the summaries measured here** — in example sessions
   (B1), outcome-triggered choice averages (largest gap 0.001, B5) and choice vs reward
   probability per task (mean gap ≤ 0.008, B6). These are weak tests: averaged P(right)
   curves are easy to match (CTT's B5-style gap is 0.014). The discriminating comparisons
   are in r2 (history kernels, trial-by-trial likelihood).
2. **The state is effectively low-dimensional and organised by the last trial.** PC1 is
   choice preference (it carries 81% of the previous-choice signal and 90% of the model's
   own logit); PC2 is whether the last trial was rewarded (74% of that signal). Together
   they split the state into four regions, one per previous choice × outcome (B3c).
3. **PC5 is a slow within-session drift**, which the session-conditioning input (constant
   within a session) cannot provide. It tracks within-session progress (r = 0.51 with trial
   position, 0.53 with the cumulative reward count, so "clock" and "reward count / satiety"
   cannot be told apart here), and it also carries the previous outcome (r = 0.33).
   *(Corrected 2026-10-26: an earlier version said it showed no ordering by outcome.)*
4. **Reward history is kept beyond the last trial, but off the main axes.** Rewards 2–5
   trials back are decodable with unique R² 0.98 → 0.30 after removing everything more
   recent explains, yet only 10–20% of their signal sits in PCs 1–4.
5. **After a reward the model's log-odds sit ~0.85 higher toward the chosen side, falling to
   ~0.7 at 10 trials** (B5) — but this is *not* the effect of one reward. Matching on logit(t)
   does not match the environment: at the same logit, rewarded right choices come from
   blocks where the right side is richer (P(R) − P(L) ≈ +0.24) and unrewarded ones from
   blocks where it is poorer (≈ −0.20), so later rewards differ for environmental reasons.
   The causal effect of one reward, from r2's counterfactual, decays from 1.0 to 0.02
   over 10 trials. B4: no reward mostly pulls the log-odds back toward 0 rather than past it.
6. **Mice occupy different parts of the same state space** (B3f): the same weights, but a
   different 4-d embedding *and* their own choice / reward histories, so this alone does
   not show the embedding causes it. Some mice never reach the extremes of PC1. This is the
   lead for phase F.

## Reading the figures

- **B4/B5 timing.** Row t's state has seen trials up to t−1 and produces the prediction for
  trial t; so the (t, t+1) pair in B4 isolates what trial t's choice and outcome did.
- **B5 top panel is matched on logit(t), and that is not enough.** Raw Δlogit per outcome
  group was dominated by regression to the mean (grouping by choice selects trials already
  leaning that way), hence the matching; but the match does not control the hidden reward
  probabilities, which differ systematically between rewarded and unrewarded trials (see
  Summary 5). Read B5's top panel as "what follows a reward in this environment", not as a
  causal kernel — r2's counterfactual is the causal one. (Corrected after review,
  2026-10-19; an earlier draft attributed the persistence to the model's own later choices.)
- **B6 stays jagged** even at steady state: each bin of P(rew|R) − P(rew|L) pools different
  probability pairs and baiting histories. (Fixed 2026-10-26: the bins split identical
  differences such as 0.4 − 0.1 and 0.7 − 0.4 by floating-point error; they are now centred on
  multiples of 0.1.) The point of the figure is the per-bin agreement
  between model and animal, not the curve shape.
- **B7 locations use activation patterns** (Σw), not decoder weights: ridge weights load on
  low-variance directions and pointed away from the PCs that visibly carry the signal.
  Pattern cosines are dominated by PC1, so that panel separates choice-like, reward-like
  and reward-probability variables but not much finer.

## Caveats

- One seed of one model. Robustness across seeds and model B is phase G, after review.
- The Coupled Baiting example (B1) is a 2019 mouse with a noisy session (likelihood 0.555);
  it is representative of that task's median length, not of its fit.
- Training mice only: held-out mice (embeddings fitted post hoc) are not analysed here.
- Random Walk (4 sessions) and 2 sessions with no task are in the pooled numbers but not in
  per-task splits.
- The condition columns from `add_candidate_variables` (previous action/outcome, history
  patterns, switch) are NaN for the first 10 trials of every session (~89k trials), so B3c's
  condition panels exclude those trials.
- Ignored trials are dropped before the model sees the data: after an ignore (4.1% of rows)
  "trial t−1" is the last *responded* trial, with no signal of the gap.
- The analysed eval split is also the split on which the best checkpoint was chosen (it
  picked the final step; eval likelihood rose at every checkpoint) — a negligible optimistic
  bias for the GRU in r2's comparison.
- "Trials since reward" (0 when t−1 was rewarded) and "trials since side chosen" (1 when
  chosen at t−1) use different zero points; with the cap at 20 they truncate one trial apart.
