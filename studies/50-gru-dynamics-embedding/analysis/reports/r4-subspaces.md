# Result 4 — subspaces of model C's state (PLAN.md phase E) — partial

**Done:** E1 (static geometry), E2 / E2b (ablations). Slow modes: r3 found the slowest modes at
the constant-input attractors largely orthogonal to the readout; they are not ablated here.

## Summary

1. **Only ~10% of the state's variance lies along the one direction that drives the current
   choice;** ~90% is held in the readout-null space.
2. **Each input lands on the axis where its variable is then read out.** The mean state
   change after a right-vs-left choice lines up with the stored previous choice (cos 0.92),
   with ΔQ (0.87) and with PC1; the change after reward-vs-no-reward lines up with the
   stored previous reward (0.99), with ΣQ (0.85) and with PC2. Part of this alignment is by
   construction — the input direction (E[Δh | R] − E[Δh | L]) and the previous-choice pattern
   (≈ E[h(t+1) | R] − E[h(t+1) | L]) share a term — so it says *where* inputs land, not that
   the dynamics are linear: B4's update maps are strongly nonlinear (reset-like after reward,
   flat for high-gain mice in F1). (D, r3, found the slow modes barely touch the readout.)
   (Revised after review, 2026-10-19: an earlier draft called this a near-linear accumulator.)
3. **The state reduces to a few shared axes:** every choice-history variable and ΔQ share one
   direction (cos 0.93–0.99, PC1), every reward-history variable and ΣQ share another (PC2),
   and trial position sits on PC5.
4. **What drives the choice vs what is only held:** choice-like variables point 18–33% along
   the readout (cos² 0.18–0.33); reward history, ΣQ and trial position point ~0% along it —
   they influence the choice only through the dynamics, not directly.

5. **Ablations (E2b) dissociate two timescales of reward memory.** Removing, at every step,
   the within-cluster variation along an axis from the carried state (replacing it by the
   mean of the state's own last-input cluster, so the state stays on its cluster) and
   comparing with 10 random 1-d and 10 random 2-d subspaces:
   - the **reward axis** carries the one-trial reward effect — removing its within-cluster
     spread costs 6× a random direction (Δ −0.081) and halves the reward effect one back
     (0.93 → 0.40);
   - the **readout coordinate** (and, equivalently, the two lobe value axes of r6) carries
     reward memory **beyond two trials** — removing it leaves lags 1–2 intact but abolishes
     lags ≥ 3 (0.29 → 0.09 / 0.04 three back; every random control keeps ≥ 0.17), at a
     likelihood cost within the random range;
   - the **choice axis** and PC5 (time) are dispensable within a cluster (Δ ≥ −0.004);
   - the pooled TDR value axis (v), which averages the two lobe directions, costs more
     likelihood than any random direction (−0.028, against at worst −0.021), but its reward
     kernel stays inside the random range (lag 3: 0.18, random 0.17–0.28).
   So short-term reward effects ride on the cluster jumps and the reward axis, and the
   reward memory beyond two trials on the readout coordinate and the two lobe value axes,
   not on v alone.
6. **Keeping only the readout coordinate works surprisingly well.** Removing all 127
   readout-null dimensions at every step (E2) leaves the model at 0.713 — a one-dimensional
   carried state, close to the best RL model (0.721), 0.021 below the full GRU.

## Notes

- **E2's global-mean ablation is not interpretable direction by direction.** Clamping one
  coordinate to the global mean while the other 127 keep their values creates states off the
  visited manifold: random typical-variance directions cost as much as meaningful ones (up to
  −0.23), and removing 127 dimensions (keeping a consistent 1-d state) costs less than
  removing one. E2b's conditional-mean replacement fixes this — random 1-d controls then cost
  only −0.006 to −0.021 — and is the version interpreted above. E2's 127-d result (point 6) is
  unaffected, since that state stays consistent.
- E2b's controls are 10 random subspaces per dimension drawn from the state covariance;
  "specific" means outside their range, not a formal test.

- A static test — decoding each variable with the readout direction projected out — was run
  and dropped: the other 127 dimensions carry every variable redundantly (the previous choice
  still decodes at R² 0.999), so removing one direction once cannot show whether the null
  space matters. That is a dynamic question for E2 (remove it at every step).
- The readout is not PC1 (|cos| 0.46) although PC1 carries the choice signal — the same
  weights-vs-patterns distinction as B7: a readout weight can load on low-variance directions.
- Input directions are averages over states; Δh also depends on where the state is (B4's
  update maps are state-dependent); D's flow fields (r3) show this state dependence.
